    About Me

    Hello! I'm Jade Ducharme, a Ph.D. candidate in physics at Brown University, where I work with Prof. Jonathan Pober on radio interferometry and 21-cm cosmology.

    I study the Epoch of Reionization – the period in the Universe's history when the first stars and galaxies were forming. The faint 21-cm hydrogen signal from that period is buried under terrestrial radio noise many orders of magnitude brighter, so much of my work is about finding and removing radio frequency interference before it corrupts a cosmological measurement.

    My recent work has involved building a Bayesian framework for flagging RFI, modeling moving interference sources (like satellites, or even airplanes), and using interferometric near-field corrections to estimate how high above the array those sources actually are.

    I earned my M.Sc. at Brown in 2025 and my B.Sc. in physics at McGill University in 2022. I've been a member of the MWA Collaboration since 2023, and of the AAS and CASCA since 2026. You can find my full publication record on Google Scholar. Outside research I coordinate and build the site for Astronomy on Tap Rhode Island. I work in French (native) and English (fluent).

    Publications

    Interference Meets Inference: Bayesian Time-Series Modeling of RFI

    Radio frequency interference is usually flagged with hard thresholds that throw away good data along with the bad. This work treats the interference as a time series to be modeled instead, using Bayesian inference to infer where it is and how strong it is, rather than simply declaring a sample contaminated.

    J. M. Ducharme, A. Li, M. J. Wilensky, and A. Liu.

    • Submitted to ApJ
    • First author

    Improved Modeling for Moving Sources of Radio Frequency Interference

    Interference from satellites and aircraft moves through the sky during an observation. This work models that motion directly and shows how it changes the flagging (i.e. masking) strategies we should be using.

    J. M. Ducharme, J. C. Pober, and M. J. Wilensky.

    • Submitted to PASA
    • First author

    Altitude Estimation of Radio Frequency Interference Sources

    Using interferometric near-field corrections to work out how high above the array an interfering source actually sits. Localizing an RFI contaminant in three-dimensional space marks an important step towards eventually subtracting it from the data entirely.

    J. M. Ducharme & J. C. Pober.

    • PASA 42, e010 (2025)
    • First author
